1. What Is Photosynthesis?

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. This process is fundamental to nearly all life on Earth because it produces both the food and the oxygen that most organisms depend on.

On-screen text from video Photosynthesis literally means "putting together with light." The word comes from the Greek photo (light) and synthesis (putting together).
Photosynthesis
The metabolic process in which organisms convert light energy, water, and carbon dioxide into glucose and oxygen. Takes place in the chloroplasts of plant cells.

2. Inputs and Outputs

The overall equation for photosynthesis can be summarized as: six molecules of carbon dioxide plus six molecules of water, in the presence of light energy, yield one molecule of glucose and six molecules of oxygen.

On-screen text from video 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
Equation — Overall Photosynthesis Reaction \[ 6\,\text{CO}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light energy}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2 \] Six molecules of carbon dioxide combine with six molecules of water. Using light energy captured by chlorophyll, the plant produces one molecule of glucose and releases six molecules of oxygen.

Notice that water molecules are split during the process, and the oxygen released comes from those water molecules — not from carbon dioxide. This was a surprising discovery when it was first confirmed using isotope tracing in the mid-20th century.

Study tip To remember the equation, think: "CO₂ and water go in, sugar and oxygen come out." The light energy is the driver, not a reactant that gets consumed in the traditional chemical sense.

3. The Light-Dependent Reactions

The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast. When light hits chlorophyll and other pigments, it excites electrons that pass through a series of proteins called the electron transport chain. This process splits water, releases oxygen, and generates ATP and NADPH — the energy carriers used in the next stage.

Thylakoid
A membrane-bound compartment inside the chloroplast where the light-dependent reactions take place. Thylakoids are often stacked into structures called grana.
Electron Transport Chain (ETC)
A series of protein complexes in the thylakoid membrane that transfer electrons from water to NADP⁺, pumping hydrogen ions to generate ATP in the process.

4. The Calvin Cycle

The Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma of the chloroplast and does not directly require light, though it depends on the ATP and NADPH produced by the light reactions. The cycle fixes carbon dioxide into an organic molecule through a series of enzyme-catalyzed reactions.

The Three Phases

Carbon fixation: The enzyme RuBisCO attaches a CO₂ molecule to a five-carbon sugar called RuBP, producing two molecules of 3-PGA (a three-carbon compound). This is the most critical step and RuBisCO is considered the most abundant enzyme on Earth.

Reduction: ATP provides energy and NADPH provides electrons to convert 3-PGA into G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate). This is where the energy from sunlight, captured in the light reactions, is transferred into an organic molecule.

Regeneration: Most of the G3P molecules are rearranged using additional ATP to regenerate RuBP so the cycle can continue. Only one out of every six G3P molecules exits the cycle to contribute to glucose production.

On-screen text from video It takes 3 turns of the Calvin cycle to produce one G3P molecule, and 6 turns to produce one glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆). That means 6 CO₂ molecules must be fixed.
Equation — Energy Cost per Glucose Molecule \[ 6\,\text{CO}_2 + 18\,\text{ATP} + 12\,\text{NADPH} \xrightarrow{6 \times \text{RuBisCO}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 18\,\text{ADP} + 18\,\text{P}_i + 12\,\text{NADP}^+ \] Each turn of the Calvin cycle consumes 3 ATP and 2 NADPH. Since 6 turns are needed to produce one glucose, the total energy cost is 18 ATP and 12 NADPH — all supplied by the light-dependent reactions.

Counting the Energy

The per-turn cost is often expressed as a simpler equation. For each CO₂ fixed, the cycle uses 3 ATP in the reduction and regeneration phases and 2 NADPH in the reduction phase:

Equation — Cost per Turn \[ \text{CO}_2 + 3\,\text{ATP} + 2\,\text{NADPH} \longrightarrow \tfrac{1}{6}\,\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 \] One turn of the Calvin cycle fixes one carbon and yields one-sixth of a glucose molecule. The fraction notation emphasizes that a single turn doesn't produce a complete sugar.
RuBisCO
Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase — the enzyme that catalyzes carbon fixation in the Calvin cycle. The most abundant protein on Earth by mass.

5. Key Takeaways

What to remember from this video

  • Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy (glucose), releasing oxygen as a byproduct.
  • It occurs in two stages: the light-dependent reactions (thylakoid membranes) and the Calvin cycle (stroma).
  • The light reactions produce ATP and NADPH by splitting water and using the electron transport chain.
  • The Calvin cycle uses ATP, NADPH, and CO₂ to build G3P, which is then used to synthesize glucose.
  • RuBisCO is the key enzyme that fixes carbon in the Calvin cycle — and it is the most abundant protein on Earth.
  • Six turns of the Calvin cycle are needed to produce one glucose molecule, consuming a total of 18 ATP and 12 NADPH.
